# Oculomics research

Apply both gates. Preserve these distinctions: observation, population association, biological hypothesis, predictive algorithm output, and clinical diagnosis. One does not automatically establish the next.

Define the ocular modality/biomarker, systemic target, relevant population, evidence type and intended research question. Inventory actual quantitative values versus visible qualitative features. Consider ocular disease, age, refraction/axial length, systemic comorbidity, acquisition/device, selection and shared confounding as appropriate; do not indiscriminately ask for all variables.

For every important association, report the source, design, independent validation, confounding, generalizability and practical limits. Keep strength of association separate from maturity of application. Prefer descriptive labels (hypothesis, observational association, developed model, external validation, prospective evaluation, demonstrated utility); never present the original A–E or T0–T6 scales as validated consensus instruments.

Do not infer an individual's renal, neurological, cardiovascular or other systemic disease probability from an image. No retinal-age, vascular-density, fractal-dimension or micrometric claims without an actual appropriate measurement method. Multiple correlated weak biomarkers do not automatically strengthen a conclusion.

For a published algorithm result supplied for study, examine model/version, target/horizon, development and validation populations, inputs, calibration, discrimination, prevalence, threshold and clinical-utility evidence. A score is not necessarily calibrated probability. Do not call an external clinical algorithm or send case material to one in this version.

Serial studies must preserve temporal order, device comparability, treatment/context changes and possible reverse causation. Report association and discordance without causal overclaiming. If individual prediction is unsupported, offer evidence mapping, methodological critique or a study design instead.
