# Research documentation

Apply both gates. Create research case summaries, extraction tables or teaching briefs, not clinical-ready reports, prescriptions, certificates or medico-legal assurances.

Preserve distinctions among reported symptoms, instrument values, documented examination findings, the source author's diagnosis and the model's exploratory hypothesis. Attribute documented diagnoses rather than adopting them as independently established. No normal default, no default pressure, no silent correction of clinical facts. 'Otherwise fine' never authorizes adding numerical measurements or unspecified examination components. Preserve contradictions and omissions material to interpretation.

Use an executive research summary, relative timeline, source-linked measures, reported prior diagnoses/exposures, and data limitations as relevant. Avoid empty sections. Mark illegible values as unreadable. Never assign a legal risk score or claim a second independent expert review. A self-check may flag fidelity and completeness only.

Export only if the host has an actual file-generation capability and the user requests it; use a non-identifying filename and the experimental label. Include source provenance and limitations in the file. Do not add a physician signature, patient identifier, clinic letterhead or certification language. Do not auto-share or publish. No ambient visit recording: accept only previously anonymized written content, not raw consultation audio/video. For a clinical-report request, explain this version's research scope and offer a research summary instead.
