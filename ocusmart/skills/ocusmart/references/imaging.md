# Exploratory imaging review

Apply both gates first. Do not issue diagnostic screening verdicts or clinical-ready reports. Use educational/research observations with source locations and image limitations. Analyze only supplied visible data; a prompt cannot supply segmentation software or validated measurements.

## OCT

Identify modality, eye if supplied, scan location, coverage, calibration and quality. Do not infer macular location from a generic B-scan. Evaluate visible vitreoretinal interface, retinal profile/layers, outer bands, subretinal compartment, RPE/Bruch complex and choroid. Distinguish not visible, not assessable and absent within the observed field. Avoid global 'normal' judgments from limited coverage.

Describe the position and reflectivity of a space before offering its interpretation. Do not equate every hyporeflective cavity with exudative fluid or every sub-RPE finding with a fluid-filled PED. Consider degenerative cavities, schisis, tubulation, shadows and segmentation artifacts as appropriate to the observed morphology and verified evidence. Give competing explanations when unresolved; do not force a finding from a checklist.

Use quantitative values only when supplied by the instrument or calculated by an actual documented method with adequate calibration. Visible scale and clear borders alone do not establish validated segmentation. Never infer volumetric fluid or a complete ETDRS thickness map from a single B-scan, even if foveal location is known. Do not synthesize missing sectors, normative colors, scan orientation, acquisition metadata or device details.

Do not regenerate a real scan with image generation to create a quantitative overlay. A workstation-looking picture is not a DICOM file, measurement or validated viewer. For requested scientific annotations, use only an available deterministic annotation method, preserve the source pixels and mark annotations as human-review hypotheses; if unavailable, describe regions in text. No external transfer of case images.

## Fundus / diabetic retinopathy

Assess gradability and field coverage first. A 4×4 grid may organize observations but is not a validated screening method and must not be confused with staging quadrants. Describe visible lesions and uncertainties; do not force yes/no answers. Ambiguity is not absence. Differentiate supported observations from etiologic interpretation; red dots do not automatically establish microaneurysms or diabetes. Any research grading schema must be explicitly sourced, appropriate to the modality and accompanied by 'ungradable'/'indeterminate' states. Do not classify center-involving edema from exudate proximity alone.

## Fields and cornea

Fields: distinguish test program, strategy, reliability, fixation, global indices, total/pattern deviation, artifacts and defect topology. Do not equate defects or index changes with confirmed disease/progression. Do not reconstruct missing pointwise thresholds from a screenshot.

Cornea: separate Placido topography from tomography and epithelial maps. Do not report pachymetry/posterior elevation from a modality that lacks it. Read device-specific indices only if present; do not invent BAD-D, ABCD or biomechanical results. No surgical clearance.

## Other modalities

OCT-A: inspect slab selection, segmentation, motion/projection artifacts and scan size; do not infer vessel density, FAZ area, flow speed or activity without proper measurements/evidence. Microperimetry: preserve coordinates, fixation and test protocol. Aberrometry: preserve pupil size, units and conventions. Specular microscopy: distinguish reported counts from qualitative appearance. Anterior-segment OCT: report only visible structures and provided or properly computed metrics; no invented pressure or lens transparency. Hess/Lancaster and ocular motility images require orientation and acquisition context; do not request identifiable face photographs. Do not offer generated Ishihara plates as a diagnostic color-vision test.

Output: research observation summary; source/quality; relevant morphology; hypotheses and contradictions; quantitative data with provenance; concrete limits. Include only applicable sections.
